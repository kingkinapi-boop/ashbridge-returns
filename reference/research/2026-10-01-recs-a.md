## CK-20 GST/HST line 101 vs revenue
Rule [fact]: Regular method line 101 is "the total amount of revenue from supplies of property and services,
including zero-rated and exempt supplies, and other revenue for the reporting period"; it excludes GST/HST and
PST. Quick method line 101 is revenue from taxable supplies "including the GST/HST"; it excludes zero-rated and
exempt supplies, supplies outside Canada, and ineligible supplies (real property, capital assets, employee or
shareholder benefit supplies, self-assessed supplies, agent or auction items, PST charged with GST).
Src: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/complete-file-instructions.html
Src: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/rc4058/quick-method-accounting-gst-hst.html
Quick method is open up to $400,000 a year of taxable supplies (associates included) [fact, RC4058]; the
remittance is a percentage of tax-included revenue; ITCs stay claimable on capital assets other than real property.
Conflict [fact]: RC4022 (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/rc4022/general-information-gst-hst-registrants.html)
lists taxable and zero-rated supplies in Canada as included and exempt supplies as excluded, while the return
instructions say exempt are included on line 101 (regular). [inference] RC4022 describes the tax base; use the
return instructions for line 101. Lead to confirm.
Reconciling item types (fixed list, [inference]):
1 HST booked gross in revenue (regular: remove tax). 2 Quick method: tax added to taxable revenue. 3 Zero-rated and
exempt revenue (in under regular, out under quick). 4 Sale of capital assets, real property, goodwill (out of
line 101 in both). 5 Revenue outside Canada or to Indian Act buyers (out under quick). 6 Timing: reporting period
is not the fiscal year, so a stub period at either end. 7 Quick-method ineligible supplies, benefit and self-assessed
supplies. 8 Other revenue not a supply (interest, grants), in under regular only. 9 Prior-period corrections.
Worked example (regular, calendar year): books revenue 520,000 = fees 480,000 + zero-rated 25,000 + gain on
equipment sale 15,000. Sum of the four line 101 figures = 505,000. Difference 15,000 = item 4.
Quick: taxable fees 100,000 x 1.13 = 113,000 on line 101.

## CK-21 Physician revenue vs OHIP remittance advice
Rule [fact]: claims are submitted within three months of the service; those received by the 18th "will typically
be processed for approval the following month"; the RA arrives "between the 5th and 7th of the month following"
processing; "payment should be on or before the 15th of the month".
Src: https://www.ontario.ca/document/resources-for-physicians/claims-submission
Src: https://www.ontario.ca/document/education-and-prevention-committee-billing-briefs/rules-regarding-claim-submission-periods
(three-month period from 1 April 2023; late claims reject as VJ7 stale dated).
RA adjustment codes 55 and 57 (net to zero, earlier account) came from a search snippet only: [inference] until a
bulletin page is opened.
Reconciling item types: 1 Services in the last weeks of the year, paid on a January or February RA. 2 Year-end
accrued receivable (billed, no RA yet). 3 Claim adjustments, rejects and write-offs (55, 57, VJ7). 4 Opening
receivable collected this year. 5 Non-OHIP income (uninsured services, stipends, other payors). 6 Cash vs accrual
book method.
Worked example: accrual revenue 400,000. RAs in the year total 372,000. Plus year-end billing paid next year
38,000, less opening receivable collected 14,000, plus rejects written off 4,000: 372,000 + 38,000 - 14,000
+ 4,000 = 400,000. [inference] expect a lag of one to two months of billing.
Not found: RA dates by solo vs group billing.

## CK-23 Wages vs T4 Summaries
Rule [fact]: "Income is reported on a T4 slip for the year in which it is paid, regardless of when it was earned."
Box 14 is salary, wages, commissions or other remuneration including taxable benefits, bonuses, retroactive pay
and vacation pay. Summary line 14 is the total of box 14 on all slips; summary totals must agree with the slips.
Src: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/payroll/completing-filing-information-returns/t4-information-employers/t4-slip.html
Src: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/payroll/completing-filing-information-returns/t4-information-employers/t4-summary.html
Src: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/rc4120/employers-guide-filing-t4-slip-summary.html
Non-calendar year end [inference]: books are accrual by fiscal year; T4s are by calendar year of pay date. A
fiscal year spans two T4 summaries, so split the payroll register by pay date. CRA pages give no splitting rule.
Reconciling item types: 1 Wages accrued at year end, paid after. 2 Prior-year accrual paid this year. 3 Taxable
benefits in box 14 booked outside wages. 4 Employer CPP, EI, EHT (in books, not in box 14). 5 Vacation pay
accrual change. 6 Owner bonus accrued vs paid. 7 Payments to non-employees (T4A) in wages. 8 Part of a
calendar year outside the fiscal year.
Worked example (FYE June 30, 2026): pay dates Jul to Dec 2025 total 90,000; Jan to Jun 2026 total 95,000;
paid in the fiscal year 185,000. Accrual at June 30, 2026 of 6,000 (paid July), prior accrual of 5,000 paid in
Jul 2025. Book expense 185,000 + 6,000 - 5,000 = 186,000. T4 2025 and T4 2026 each include other-half pay dates
outside the fiscal year, so only the pay-date split is comparable.

## CK-24 Dividends paid vs T5 slips
Rule [fact]: T5 is due the last day of February after the calendar year; no slip if the total to one recipient is
under $50. Eligible: box 24 actual, 25 taxable (38% gross-up), 26 credit. Other than eligible: box 10 actual, 11
taxable, 12 credit. Boxes 11 and 25 are not completed for corporate recipients. Box 18 is capital gains dividends
(funds), not s.83(2) capital dividends. T5 Summary totals are the slip totals.
Src: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/t4015/t5-guide-return-investment-income.html
Src: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/completing-slips-summaries/financial-slips-summaries/return-investment-income-t5/t5-summary.html
Src: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/completing-slips-summaries/financial-slips-summaries/return-investment-income-t5/t5-slip/completing-t5-slip.html
[inference] Compare boxes 10 plus 24 (actual), never the grossed-up boxes 11 and 25.
Capital dividend [fact]: elected dividend is generally outside shareholder Part I income; T2054 is due by the
earlier of the day payable and the first day paid. T5 treatment not stated: [not found], flag for a person.
Src: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/technical-information/income-tax/income-tax-folios-index/series-3-property-investments-savings-plans/series-3-property-investments-savings-plan-folio-2-dividends/income-tax-folio-s3-f2-c1-capital-dividends.html
Reconciling item types: 1 Declared in one calendar year, paid in the next. 2 Credited to a shareholder loan account
(paid or credited) vs cash. 3 Recipients under $50. 4 Capital dividends. 5 Deemed dividends (s.84, PUC reduction,
redemption) on a T5 but not booked as dividends. 6 Non-resident recipients (NR4, not researched). 7 Set-off against
a shareholder loan. 8 Amended or late slips.
Worked example (FYE Mar 31, 2026, other than eligible): paid Apr to Dec 2025 40,000; Jan to Mar 2026 20,000; books
60,000. T5 2025 box 10 = 40,000 + 15,000 paid Jan to Mar 2025 (prior fiscal year) = 55,000; T5 2026 so far 20,000.
Match: 60,000 = (55,000 - 15,000) + 20,000.

## CK-25 HST balance vs net tax per period
Rule [fact]: net tax = GST/HST collected or collectible (line 103) minus ITCs (line 106), with adjustments
(104, 107); 105 and 108 are calculated (return layout from 13 May 2024). Example on the page: 1,000 collected,
800 ITCs, net tax 200.
Src: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/complete-file-return-business/calculate-net-tax.html
[inference] Year-end balance = net tax for each period ending on or before year end and not yet paid, plus any CRA
balance (interest, penalties), minus any refund receivable. Negative net tax is a receivable.
Reconciling item types: 1 Net tax of unremitted periods at year end. 2 Payments made after year end for earlier
periods. 3 CRA interest and penalties (not tax). 4 Stub period straddling year end (pre-year-end part only).
5 Quick method: payable is a percent of tax-included revenue, the gap to tax collected is income. 6 Rebates
(employee and partner, s.253). 7 Instalments. 8 Adjustments reported in later returns.
Worked example (quarterly, FYE Dec 31): Q4 collectible 11,300, ITCs 4,100, net tax 7,200 due Jan 31. Q3 paid
before year end. CRA interest 40. Expected HST payable 7,240. Books show 7,900: unexplained 660, flag.

## CK-26 Dividends declared (GIFI 3700) vs Schedule 3 plus capital dividends
Not verified: GIFI code 3700 meaning and Schedule 3 line numbers (guide page 404). [inference] 3700 is dividends
declared on the retained earnings reconciliation; Schedule 3 reports taxable dividends paid in the year; capital
dividends are elected under s.83(2) with T2054 and are not taxable dividends. Folio URL as in CK-24 for the
election. CDA overview (search result only, not opened):
https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account/capital-dividend-accounts.html
Reconciling item types: 1 Declared in year, unpaid at year end. 2 Declared in prior year, paid this year. 3 Capital
dividends paid. 4 Stock dividends or deemed dividends. 5 Dividends credited to a shareholder loan. 6 Eligible vs
other than eligible reclassification (no total effect).
Worked example: 3700 = 120,000 (taxable 100,000, capital 20,000). 15,000 of the taxable was declared Dec 20 and
paid Jan 5; a prior-year 30,000 declaration was paid this year. Taxable paid = 100,000 - 15,000 + 30,000 =
115,000; plus capital 20,000 = 135,000 vs 3700 of 120,000; difference 15,000 = -15,000 + 30,000.

## Three most important findings
1 Line 101 flips on tax: regular excludes tax and includes zero-rated and exempt; quick includes tax and excludes both.
2 T4 box 14 and T5 follow the pay or credit date, so a non-calendar year end always needs a pay-date split.
3 OHIP: 18th cut-off, RA on the 5th to 7th, payment by the 15th of the next month, so one to two months of lag.
