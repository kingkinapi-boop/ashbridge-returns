## Item 37 Quick method, restaurant, line 101, 1% credit
- [fact] RC4058: line 101 = "revenues from taxable supplies (include the GST/HST ...)", so tax-included. (RC4058)
- [fact] Ontario 13% HST supplies: service providers 8.8%, goods resellers 4.4%. (RC4058)
- [fact] Reseller rate needs goods bought in the prior fiscal year for resale "or to use in goods you produce or manufacture for sale" to be at least 40% of total tax-included annual taxable-supply revenue. (RC4058)
- [fact] RC4058 lists "fast-food outlets", "caterers", "delicatessens" as service providers; it names no "restaurant" and no restaurant treatment. (RC4058)
- [fact] 1% credit: on the first 30,000 of tax-included eligible supplies, each fiscal year, only if the election was in effect at the start of that fiscal year; it is deducted in working out net tax (line 107). (RC4058)
- [fact] Eligibility cap of 400,000 is tax-included ("revenues (including the GST/HST) ... not more than $400,000"). (RC4058) Sample 04's 415,310.24 tax-included is over it: [inference] the sample's own eligibility needs a person's look (item 37 does not ask this; flag for the CPA).
- [fact] Practitioner pages apply 8.8% to Ontario restaurants and compare quick vs regular each year: https://gondaliyacpa.ca/gst-hst-filing-for-restaurants/ (opened; says 8.8%, silent on the credit). The firm page https://www.modernaxis.ca/blog/gst-hst-quick-method was NOT opened; do not cite it.
- Answer [inference]: a sit-down restaurant uses 8.8% unless the 40% food-cost test is met, which is rare (food cost usually 30 to 35%; that figure came from a search snippet only, [guess]). The test world's K9 "retailer" label should not decide the rate: the rate follows the 40% test. Credit: due, if the election was in force at the start of the fiscal year; the sample's remittance omitting it is an error. Line 101 tax-included: yes, so 415,310.24 as item 37 says.
- Preparer checks: rate class from the prior-year cost-of-goods ratio; election date; 400,000 cap; credit entered. Client asked: nothing new (revenue and purchases from books). CPA: choice of method.
- Confidence: line 101 and credit high; restaurant = service rate medium (inference from the 40% test and the fast-food example).

## Item 38 Passive income 64,800 and the year of the cut
- [fact] AAII includes dividends from non-connected corporations ("Additions: Dividends from non-connected corporations"); the business limit is reduced by AAII earned in each taxation year ending in the preceding calendar year. https://www.bccpa.ca/kbase/kbase-search/taxation/taxation/articles/holding-passive-investments-in-a-private-corporation/ (opened; an older article, 2018 examples).
- [fact, snippet-level, from search results page not opened as a source] 50,000 threshold, 5 to 1, nil at 150,000. Not cited: the CRA page I tried returned 404. Confirm on canada.ca or ITA 125(5.1).
- [inference] Whether 64,800 is exactly right depends on the other lines in sample 05 (AAII nets out some deductions and takes only taxable capital gains); I cannot check without the key. Dividends from connected corporations are excluded, so non-connected is the one that counts.
- Timing [inference]: the rule is by calendar year of the year-end, not "the next tax year". A Dec 2025 year-end AAII cuts the limit for years ending in 2026; a non-December year-end can be hit in the very next year or in a year that begins later. State it as "years ending in the next calendar year".
- Preparer: Schedule 7 AAII lines; CPA: associated-corporation AAII and planning. Client asked: investment statements only. Confidence: dividends included high; year rule high; exact 64,800 low.

## Item 40 Amount due from shareholder, interest paid
- [fact] Practitioner view: year-end shareholder account review is of the books, with attention to amounts taken out, personal expenses and loans; a firm says decide compensation mix with the accountant before the year, not by default. https://www.connectcpa.ca/blog/shareholder-loans-the-one-year-rule-and-the-subsection-15-2-trap (opened; does not say what is asked of clients).
- [fact] Interest benefit under 80.4 is cut if the shareholder pays interest at the prescribed rate (3% Q2 2026 per that page) within 30 days of year end. Same page.
- [inference] Practice: the balance comes from the books (shareholder loan account); the client is asked to confirm it and the interest paid (bank record, since the payment is often outside the books), and whether it was repaid after year-end (15(2.6) one-year rule). The CPA decides treatment. Safe design: books first, then a confirm-and-add-interest question to the client; the number is not the client's to set.
- Confidence: medium (no firm page opened that lists the client question).

## Item 42 T4A and T5018 at year end
- [fact] T4A box 048 fees for services: issue if total payments in the calendar year are more than 500 (CRA administrative policy), excluding GST/HST; a corporation with construction as main business uses T5018 instead. https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/payroll/completing-filing-information-returns/t4a-information-payers/t4a-slip.html (opened)
- [fact] CRA says it is not assessing penalties for box 048 except the trucking industry (from 2025). https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/programs/about-canada-revenue-agency-cra/compliance/reporting-fees-for-service.html (opened)
- [fact, snippet only, not opened] T5018: more than 50% construction income, subcontractors, 500 threshold, six months after period end. Not cited as a source; the canada.ca T5018 page URL I guessed returned 404.
- Answer [inference]: yes, a year-end check should prompt for both: a T4A/T5018 question when subcontractor or contractor expense exists (screen by expense accounts). It is a prompt for the preparer, not a block, since penalties for T4A box 048 are mostly suspended. Confidence: medium.
