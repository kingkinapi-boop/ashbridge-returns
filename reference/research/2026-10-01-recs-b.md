## CK-20 Revenue vs line 101
- [fact S3] Regular: line 101 is revenue from supplies including zero-rated and exempt supplies and other revenue, excluding GST/HST, PST and amounts reported on a previous return. Quick method: revenue from taxable supplies including the GST/HST; zero-rated, real property sales and self-assessed items excluded.
- [fact S2] Practice: line 101 should reconcile to gross revenue on the T2; CRA audits where they differ. Quick method makes a deliberate difference.
- [fact S1] QBO tool compares P&L income to line 101 and lists (a) income coded out of scope, blank or purchase-type tax codes (some out-of-scope is legitimate; often a coding error); (b) amounts in line 101 but not in P&L income (bad-debt credit memos, deferred income or gift vouchers on balance sheet accounts). Differences are not automatically errors.
- [inference] Usual reconciling items: income outside line 101 (interest, gain on disposal, grants); exempt or out-of-scope supplies on the P&L; fiscal year vs filing periods straddling year end; invoices in a period not yet filed; credit notes, bad debts; deferred revenue; Quick-method tax in revenue.
- Normally unexplained: a few dollars of rounding. Larger residue is a flag for a person.
- False alarms: fiscal year vs GST periods; Quick method; prior-year sales reported late.
- Example (regular method, Jan to Dec): P&L revenue 412,000; less interest 1,800 and gain on van 6,500 (outside line 101 supplies, person confirms); sum of four returns line 101 = 403,700. 412,000 - 8,300 = 403,700. Unexplained 0.

## CK-21 Physician revenue vs OHIP remittance advice
- [fact S4 S5] Reconcile claims submitted, payer's adjudication, amount received. The RA shows paid, reduced, rejected, held, adjusted or recovered. Corrections, rejections and stale-dated claims lengthen timelines; a claim may miss that month's cycle.
- [fact S5] Expected payment = payable fee + premiums or adjustments - reductions, exclusions or recoveries.
- [inference] Usual reconciling items: services rendered but unpaid at year end (OHIP receivable); prior-year receivable received this year; rejected or held claims; recoveries and clawbacks; non-OHIP income (WSIB, private, uninsured, other contracts) kept in separate accounts.
- Normally unexplained: small reductions with no line detail; held claims with unknown outcome.
- False alarms: December billings paid in January; RA date vs bank deposit date.
- Example: books revenue 520,000. Twelve RAs total 488,000. Receivable year end 71,000, prior-year receivable collected 39,000. 488,000 + (71,000 - 39,000) = 520,000. Unexplained 0. If books say 524,000, 4,000 goes to a person.

## CK-22 Cash, loans and cards vs statements
- [fact S6] Standard items: outstanding cheques, deposits in transit, bank fees and interest appearing only on the statement, NSF cheques, correction entries; leftover differences are entry slips, omissions, duplicates, bank errors.
- [inference] Loans: lender balance vs books, with accrued interest and payments in transit. Cards: statement date often mid-month, so books exceed statement by charges after the statement date.
- Normally unexplained: nothing; after listed items the difference is zero, otherwise an entry is missing.
- False alarms: statement date not year end; pending authorisations; foreign currency.
- Example: bank per statement 31 Dec 48,200; deposits in transit +3,100; outstanding cheques -5,450; adjusted bank 45,850. Books 45,895 less unbooked bank fee 45 = 45,850. Unexplained 0.

## CK-23 Wages vs T4 Summaries
- [fact S7] T4 is cash basis: pay periods paid in the calendar year, not earned. The T4 Summary totals slips and reconciles deductions to remittances.
- [fact S9] Payroll entries hit bank, wage and employer-tax expense, CPP/EI/tax liabilities.
- [inference] Usual reconciling items: wages accrued at year end and paid after; fiscal year spanning two calendar years (so use every T4 Summary for those years, and split by pay date); accrued bonuses; taxable benefits on T4 with no cash; employer CPP/EI in the P&L but not in box 14; vacation pay accrual; wages posted to cost of sales or other accounts.
- Normally unexplained: small benefit valuation differences.
- False alarms: calendar vs fiscal year; owner paid by dividend only (no T4 is legitimate).
- Example: fiscal year 1 Jul to 30 Jun. P&L wages 190,000. Pay dated 1 Jul to 31 Dec per register 88,000; 1 Jan to 30 Jun 94,000; accrued wages at 30 Jun 8,000. 88,000 + 94,000 + 8,000 = 190,000. T4 summaries then total box 14 for each calendar year, and the pay dates tie the split. Unexplained 0.

## CK-24 Dividends paid vs T5
- [fact S8] T5 box 10 actual non-eligible, box 11 grossed up 15%; box 24 actual eligible, box 25 grossed up 38%. Gross-up boxes are for individuals.
- [inference] Compare actual boxes (10 and 24) not 11 and 25, to dividends in the equity ledger by calendar year; capital dividends have no T5 box and are listed separately. Reconciling items: gross-up (avoid by using actual boxes); credited to shareholder loan (still paid); declared in one calendar year and paid in the next; dividends to a corporate shareholder (no gross-up slip fields); slip not yet filed for the latest year.
- Normally unexplained: none.
- False alarms: December declaration paid January; comparing grossed-up box with ledger.
- Example: ledger 60,000 (non-eligible 40,000, eligible 20,000). T5 box 10 40,000 (box 11 46,000), box 24 20,000 (box 25 27,600). 40,000 + 20,000 = 60,000. Unexplained 0.

## CK-25 HST balance vs net tax
- [fact S3] Net tax = line 105 - line 108; instalments (110) and rebates (111) reduce the amount owing.
- [inference] Books HST payable at year end vs net tax for unfiled or unremitted periods, plus any CRA balance. Reconciling items: ITCs on year-end payables not yet booked; HST on invoices accrued; instalments booked as expense; interest and penalties; payment in transit; Quick-method differences (tax portion of revenue, 1% credit as other income); self-assessments.
- Normally unexplained: rounding only.
- False alarms: payment made days before year end that CRA applies later; CRA statement lag.
- Example: Q4 net tax 3,420 unfiled; Q3 balance 2,100 paid 28 Dec, cleared 3 Jan. Books HST payable 5,520. Net tax 3,420 + CRA balance 2,100 (payment in transit). Unexplained 0.

## CK-26 Dividends declared vs Schedule 3
- [not found] A practice source on reconciling declared (GIFI 3700) to Schedule 3.
- [inference] Reconciling items: declared but unpaid (dividends payable); prior-year declaration paid this year; capital dividends (on Schedule 3 but not taxable); credited to shareholder loan.
- Example: declared 75,000. Schedule 3 taxable dividends paid 50,000 + capital dividends 10,000 = 60,000; declared but unpaid 15,000. 60,000 + 15,000 = 75,000. Unexplained 0.

## Proposed fixed reconciling-item types (all [inference])
1. timing_period (fiscal vs calendar or filing period)
2. timing_payment_lag (cleared or paid after year end)
3. accrual_unpaid (earned or declared, not yet paid)
4. prior_period_settled (prior-year accrual settled this year)
5. statement_date_gap
6. outstanding_item (cheque, deposit in transit)
7. out_of_scope_income (interest, gains, grants, non-OHIP income)
8. tax_treatment (exempt, zero-rated, quick method, gross-up, benefits)
9. offset_loan (settled against a shareholder loan or account)
10. fee_or_interest_unbooked
11. adjustment_or_recovery (credit note, bad debt, OHIP recovery)
12. rounding (within tolerance)
13. other_explained (free text, needs a document reference)
Each item carries amount, source document and who accepted it. The remainder is "unexplained": code flags a person, never clears it.
