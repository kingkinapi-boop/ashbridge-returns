## Method (to repeat it)

For every `.jsonl` (main sessions at the top level, helpers under `<session>/subagents/`, with a `.meta.json` giving the helper's description and type): take each assistant message once (dedupe by `message.id`) and read `message.usage`. Context per turn = `input_tokens + cache_creation_input_tokens + cache_read_input_tokens`. `output_tokens` in the logs is recorded mid-stream and is not reliable. A "full re-write" is a turn with more than 50k cache-creation tokens and more written than read.

2. Fact. Context per turn is large: Lead session medians 255k to 458k, peaks 781k, 524k and 514k (the 781k session passed the watcher's 65%). 5. Fact. 62 full re-writes in helpers, 11.6M tokens; 59 of them came after a gap of more than 5 minutes. The command before the gap: `until ...` wait loops 5.7M, Read 0.8M, `tools/heavy.mjs` slot waits about 0.6M, others small. Local queue workers re-wrote 6 to 15 times their peak context.
6. Fact (Claude Code docs, via a guide helper): subagent prompt cache lives 5 minutes (the main session gets 1 hour); a pause longer than that re-writes the whole prompt. `CLAUDE_AUTOCOMPACT_PCT_OVERRIDE` (env-vars doc) sets the compaction point and applies to subagents; whether cloud sessions honour it is not documented. Agent frontmatter has no effort field; it has `maxTurns` and `experimental.cacheTtl` (v2.1.248 and later; this laptop runs 2.1.286).
7. Inference. Replaying every session's per-turn context with a limit (after the limit, a fresh start at 45k to 90k plus 8 to 15 turns to re-orient): at 200k the Lead reads 50 to 58% less and helpers 27 to 32% less; overall about a third less for the same work. At 250k: Lead 46 to 52%, helpers 21 to 24%.
8. Inference. A full re-write costs about twelve times a cache read of the same size (published cache prices: write 1.25 times input, read 0.1 times). Waiting in steps under 5 minutes is cheaper than one re-write for any wait under about an hour.
9. Fact. Secrets: no key-like string (GitHub, Anthropic, AWS, Slack, Google, private keys) in tracked files or local transcripts. The one history hit is a planted fake AWS key in TH's gitleaks rule test (commit f1c8095, branch claude/TH). `.env` is git-ignored. `.claude/settings.json` env holds only model names.
