# T2 practice and tools: are we reinventing wheels, and what is missing?

Research, 29 Sep 2026. Read-only. Plan read: blueprint 00, 03, 05, 11 in full; 01, 02, 04, 09 and README in full; onboarding-contract, design-2026-09-28, sources.md, TODO-ZO by reading or grep; cards by grep of slices.json.
Labels: [fact, TAG] has a source in the list at the end. [inference] is my reading of facts. [guess] has no source. "Not found" means I looked and found nothing.

## Answer in brief

1. Reinvented wheel 1: the books layer (TB-1 to TB-9: working trial balance, adjusting entries, GIFI mapping, roll-forward). The blueprint README calls it "the largest build item". [fact, CW1, CW3, CCH14, IN1] CaseWare Working Papers and ReviewComp, CCH iFirm Engagement and QuickBooks Workpapers all ship a trial balance with adjusting entries and GIFI export, and CCH links Engagement to Taxprep [CCH15]. None of them, in the pages I opened, gives a machine-checked source pointer per entry. [inference] That pointer is the only part worth building.
2. Reinvented wheel 2: CRA data capture by a manual PDF checklist (RV-30, CK-12, CK-13). [fact, CCH9, CRA12, TC3] Taxprep's own "TaxprepConnect download" is CRA's T2 Auto-fill: return data, account balances for five years, the notice of assessment, with differences from the file highlighted; CRA's service also carries loss balances, GRIP, RDTOH and the capital dividend account.
3. Reinvented wheel 3: the plain ties CK-10 and CK-11. [fact, CRA9, CCH7, CCH11] CRA itself validates assets = liabilities + equity and revenue - expenses = net income, and Taxprep runs 3,000+ diagnostics, one type of which exists so the return is not rejected.
4. Not reinvented (nothing found): the cell trace with orphans and overrides, evidence dots, approval fingerprints, code-checked AI citations, and an AI reviewer for Canadian T2s. [fact, AI1] The AI return-review tools I found (CPA Pilot, Agent Andrew, Ready to Review) read US returns only; the Canadian AI tools are research (CCH AnswerConnect, Blue J, TaxGPT) or bookkeeping (Intuit, Xero).
5. Biggest gaps, in order: (1) the income tax provision loop; (2) T4 and T5 slip preparation; (3) financial statements, notes, compilation and Schedule 141; (4) loss, donation and reserve claims and next year's instalments; (5) corporations outside Ontario; (6) two rule slips: CK-33 is one day off and FLOW-7 skips CRA's last-day rule. Details in section 4.
6. Path in section 6: one real-Taxprep spike with made-up data first; one red amendment (gaps 1 to 3, 5 and the two rule fixes); switch RV-30 to Auto-fill; accept CaseWare, iFirm Engagement and Workpapers exports as the books.

| 100, 125, 141 | GIFI balance sheet, income statement, notes checklist. Taxprep also lists 140 (income statement summary) and 101 (opening balance sheet, new corporations) | always; inactive corporations are excused | CRA3, CRA7, CCH5 |

| 8 | Capital cost allowance | depreciable property | CRA4 |
| 50, 9, 23, 7 | Holders of 10% or more; related and associated; business limit agreement; investment income and SBD income | private; associated; CCPC | CRA3, CRA5, CRA6 |
| 3, 53 | Dividends received, taxable dividends paid, Part IV tax; GRIP | dividends | CRA6, CRA20 |

- [inference] No CRA usage counts by schedule found. From the lists, 1, 8, 50, 100, 125, 141 and 500 sit on almost every active return; 7, 9, 23 on CCPCs with the SBD; 3 and 53 on dividend payers. A CCPC with nil income or a loss may use the two-page T2 short return [fact, CRA1].
- Due dates [fact]: file six months after year end, on the last day of the month if the year end is the last day of a month, otherwise the same day [CRA1]. Balance due two months; three for a CCPC that claimed the SBD (current or previous year) and whose previous year's taxable income (for a group, the last year ending in the previous calendar year) is within its business limit(s) [CRA2]. Late filing 5% of unpaid tax plus 1% a month, 12 months at most; e-filing is required for tax years starting after 2023 with four exceptions [CRA1, CRA12]. T5 by the last day of February, penalty $100 to $7,500, e-file if more than 5 slips [CRA22]; the T4 date should match but its CRA page returned 404 [guess]. Instalments not needed if tax payable is $3,000 or less; quarterly needs a CCPC with perfect compliance, taxable income (group) of $500,000 or less and taxable capital of $10 million or less [CRA21].
- GIFI [fact]: same level of detail as the statements, never only subtotals; codes 1000 to 3849 for the balance sheet, 7000 to 9970 income, 9975 to 9999 taxes; CRA checks assets = liabilities + equity and revenue - expenses = net income, and requires items 2599, 3499, 3620, 8299, 9368 and 9999, plus 3849 when retained earnings are broken down [CRA9]. Current income tax is 9990, deferred tax 9995, taxes payable 2680; in CRA's worked example 9999 = 9970 + 9980 - 9990, and 3680 must equal 9999 plus or minus 9998 [CRA9]. Taxprep moves GIFI amounts into the forms when the preparer answers Yes to transfer [CCH5].
- Where CRA adjusts [fact]: the Income Tax Audit Manual chapter 24 covers benefits conferred on shareholders (personal expenses paid by the company), shareholder debt (15(2), 80.4 interest benefit) and transfers to a corporation [CRA13]; loans repaid within one year after the lender's tax year end are excluded, unless a series of loans and repayments [CRA16]; a personal services business (five or fewer full-time employees, employee-like) loses the SBD and the general reduction and pays 5% extra, and CRA's pilots found most of them in transportation and warehousing and in professional services [CRA14, CRA15]; auditors read owners' personal bank and card statements and related-party records [CRA25]; risk flags include lifestyle not matching income and a high-risk sector [CRA17].
- Weaker sources: practitioner blogs list revenue that does not match HST returns, odd expense ratios, shareholder loans, vehicles, travel and meals, contractor versus employee, missing T slips, non-deductible items, family payments, mixed-use expenses and corporate donations [AU1]. Not found: an official ranked list of the most frequent T2 reassessment items.

## 2. What small firms actually do

- Compilation [fact]: one Canadian firm calls it "the most common type of financial statement engagement for small businesses in Canada" [CP3]. CSRS 4200 replaced Notice to Reader [CP3]; it applies to periods ending on or after 14 Dec 2021 and its report states the basis of accounting and the split of duties [CP1]. The CPA Canada and CaseWare PEG template carries the file: engagement letter, knowledge of the entity, management acknowledgment, compilation report, completion checklist [CW5]. BC CPA's 2024-25 review still found old Notice to Reader reports, missing or wrong basis-of-accounting notes (ASPE claimed without a cash flow statement), incomplete engagement letters and thin independence records [CP2].
- Money [fact]: T2 flat fee $1,500 to $6,000 depending on complexity and whether statements are included; compilation $500 to $2,500 as its own line [FEE1]; Ontario boutique corporate tax $2,000 to $8,000 [FEE1]. One hybrid firm sells "Financial Statements for Tax Purposes" and a "Compilation Engagement Report" as two products [PR1]. Ashbridge's own client app lists "compilation" as a separate service on the same corporation [onboarding-contract.md line 21].
- Tools [fact]: year-end trial balances go into CaseWare Working Papers or ReviewComp, CCH iFirm Engagement or QuickBooks Workpapers; statements and report are drafted there; a GIFI file goes out to Taxprep, TaxCycle or ProFile; the return is filed from CRA-certified software (14 vendors on CRA's list) [CW1, CW3, CCH14, IN1, CRA11]. [inference] Which one a small firm uses follows its books system; I found no market-share data.
- How GIFI relates to the statements [fact]: GIFI is the statements coded; CRA wants the same line detail [CRA9]. Paper statements are not needed when e-filing, and notes and the accountant's report can be sent with the e-file [CRA3]; the older guide copy says notes must be included if prepared [CRA9]. Schedule 141 records who prepared the statements: 1 audit, 2 review, 3 compilation; parts 2 and 3 are skipped if the accountant has no designation or is connected [CRA9]. Not found: guidance for a GIFI-only engagement with no statements delivered; BC CPA says CSRS 4200 may be applied even with no third-party user [CP2].
- Time [not found]: no authoritative hours per return. Practitioner step order: close books and adjust, map GIFI, Schedule 1, Schedule 8, other schedules, tax, e-file [PR1]. Cost falls with clean books and organized documents [FEE1]. [inference] The long steps are book cleanup, chasing documents and reconciling; one firm calls Schedule 1 "the most technically complex" [PR1]. [guess] Fees of $1,500 to $6,000 at $150 to $300 an hour bracket a file at about 5 to 40 hours, too wide to plan on.

## 4. Cross-check of our plan

(a) Common small-CCPC items the plan does not cover. Blueprint grep for provision, 9990, prepaid, Schedule 141, compilation, Alberta, Quebec and province found nothing (CK-11 mentions 3680 only).
- A1 Income tax provision loop. [fact] Net income after tax (9999) needs 9990, retained earnings roll from it (CK-11) and taxes payable is 2680 [CRA9]; Schedule 1 adds the provision back on lines 101 and 102 [CRA10]. [inference] So it closes in one pass: import pre-tax GIFI, read tax from the review-lines export (RT-10), book Dr 9990 and Cr 2680 (instalments already paid sit against 2680), re-import, re-lock. Without it CK-11 fails or passes only because TB-5 forces it. Fix: one entry type, one lifecycle step, one check.
- A2 T4 and T5 slips. [fact] The client app collects payroll people and slip recipients and its Q&A rule uses a "February slip date" [onboarding-contract.md lines 46, 60, 91]; the blueprint only checks slips (CK-23, 24, 38); card E22 reads T5s. Taxprep Forms prepares T4 and T5 and the example row in CCH's own CSV syntax page is a T4 slip [CCH2, CCH16]. Fix: name it in 11-not-building as a person's job, or add a slip module on the same CSV writer.
- A3 Financial statements, notes, compilation. [fact] No statement output, no CSRS 4200 file and no owner for Schedule 141 answers (card M22 maps the cells only). [inference] If the firm compiles, the working TB is the compile file, so TB, statements and report should be one product. [inference] RT-15 needs Schedule 141 on the allowed-typing list, or the answers need a source.
- A4 Loss, donation and reserve claims. [fact] TB-6 names CCA, dividend designations, elections and business limit shares (if these are examples, add the rest); CK-12 ties only opening pools; cards M19 and M20 map Schedules 4 and 2; Schedules 6 and 13 have no card. [inference] Missing: judgment inputs for loss use and carryback, donations and reserves, and a "claim within the pool" check.
- A5 Next year's instalments. [fact] Only an AI checklist item (AI-2, card I20). [inference] The $3,000 test and the monthly or quarterly test are code [CRA21]; RULE-17 says code before AI.
- A6 Year-end adjustments. [fact] TB-2 has an accrual type but no checklist (prepaids, accrued liabilities, amortization, bad debts, payroll accrual, provision). Only CK-33 checks an accrual. Not covered: amounts owed to non-arm's-length persons that stay unpaid past the end of the second following year and then come into income in the third (78(1)) [CRA24].
- A7 Outside Ontario. [fact] The brief shows Ontario tax (RV-2); no gate. CRA administers every province but Quebec and Alberta, which need separate returns [CRA19]; Schedule 5 is CRA's supplementary provincial form [CRA18]. [inference] It carries the allocation when a corporation has permanent establishments in several provinces. Also unnamed: Ontario Schedules 500, 502, 511 and the credit list (R&D, innovation, co-op education, manufacturing) [CRA18, CRA20], rare for small CCPCs [inference]: flag, do not build. Fix: a permanent establishment outside Ontario, or in Quebec or Alberta, is red tier and a person's file.
- A8 Schedules with no mapping card. [fact] Cards map 1, 2, 3, 4, 7, 8, 9 and 23, 50, 53, 100, 125, 141 and "Ontario schedules". Not carded: 6, 11, 13, 88, 101, 140, T2 short. [inference] K8 (shareholder loan) needs 11 and K3 (first year) needs 101. Add them or list them as "person types it".
- A9 Test world. [inference] No nil or dormant return, no loss year, no rental company, no outside-Ontario company among K1 to K13.
- A10 Tax-law upkeep. [fact] Taxprep's 2026 notes add a $39,000 Class 10.1 limit, reaccelerated investment incentive CCA and an Ontario Schedule 572 rate change [CCH13]. [inference] A home-made rule library needs a yearly owner; lean on Taxprep for tax law and use our checks for document ties.

(b) Plan items an existing tool already does well: the books layer (wheel 1), CRA capture (Auto-fill), GIFI file import, CK-10 and CK-11 (CRA validity checks), statement text extraction (Dext and converters, without box data), T183CORP signing (already reused). See section 5.

(c) Plan items that look over-built for a small firm [inference unless marked]:
- Four exports and two fingerprint gates per return and a fault-injecting simulator (RT-1 to RT-23). [fact] CCH's import already prints a report of changed and failed cells [CCH1] and locking stops edits (RULE-2). Export 1 partly repeats that report; export 0 mainly classifies rolled-forward cells (RT-14). Start with exports 2 and 3.
- Our own lead sheets, sign-offs and roll-forward inside the TB layer (they exist in Engagement and Working Papers).
- CK-10, 11 and 15 before real files: Taxprep's GIFI transfer feeds the forms [CCH5], and its diagnostics and CRA's validity checks cover the balance ties [CCH7, CRA9]. The Schedule 1 add-back tie (CK-15) is probably covered by the same transfer; confirm in the spike. Keep CK-18 (the trace) and CK-20 to 26.
- Learning-loop ranking and cause attribution before there is volume (version capture is cheap; ranking is not).
- A second-model red team plus a story check on day one; the US tools sell a single pass over the return PDF [AI1]. Note the design says "one CPA, thousands of returns", so review automation itself is justified; it is the foundation size that is in doubt.

(d) Plan rules I checked against sources:
- CK-33 [fact, CRA24, AU1]: ITA 78(4) tests remuneration "unpaid on the day that is 180 days after the end of the taxation year", so pay by day 179. CK-33 and K9 say "within 180 days". sources.md lists 78(4) as unchecked; now checked.
- FLOW-7 says "six months after year end". [fact, CRA1] CRA uses the last-day rule (year end 28 Feb gives 31 Aug). Balance-due text matches CRA [CRA2].
- Match CRA: CK-20 quick method line 101 [CRA23]; CK-30 and CK-31 [CRA16]; CK-19 short-year proration and the 50,000 to 150,000 passive band [CRA5]. Not confirmed by pages I opened: CK-36's Ontario point (a search result says Ontario decoupled on 15 Nov 2018; sources.md cites a page I did not open).

## 7. Limits of this research

- CPA Canada and CPA Ontario pages returned 403; CSRS 4200 facts come from the AASB user brochure, BC CPA pages and firm pages. The full standard was not read.
- Wolters Kluwer product pages returned 403; the claim "750 GIFI items into 25 forms" is unverified. Taxprep help pages read are for Corporate Taxprep; iFirm Taxprep is assumed to match. Taxprep's own Auto-fill page does not itemise loss, GRIP or RDTOH balances; CRA's page does; the spike should confirm what lands in Taxprep.
- Xero Central's GIFI page returned empty. Caseware AiDA and the Extractly purchase date were not verified. The RC4088 copy is Rev. 21, hosted by CCH, so it may trail CRA's current guide.
- Not found: hours per return, official ranked reassessment items, any Canadian AI reviewer for T2 returns, whether Ontario Schedule 511 binds a small associated group.
