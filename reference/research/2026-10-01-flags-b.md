## Cross-cutting findings
- [fact] Practice sources (small-firm blogs, no CPA Canada or CTF page opened) all name the same audit adjustments: shareholder loan balances, personal expenses booked as business, vehicle/meals/home office, unexplained year-over-year swings. https://segalgcse.com/insights/personal-tax/red-flags-cra-audit/ , https://huangcpas.com/top-cra-audit-triggers-for-canadian-small-businesses-2026-guide/
- [fact] CRA finds loans and benefits by reading bank and credit card statements, not only the shareholder loan account. https://mackisen.com/blog/cra-shareholder-loan-audits-section-15(2)-assessments-personal-benefit-reassessments-and-corporate-enforcement-a-montreal-cpa-firm-guide
- [fact] "Courts expect strong documentation, not verbal explanations" (same page). So every flag needs a written reason stored with the flag.
- [inference] Documentation pattern for the reviewer note on every flag: (1) the fact seen, with source row; (2) the test applied; (3) the conclusion (cleared / adjusted / carried to next year); (4) the evidence kept (resolution, agreement, log). Four fields, all required to clear.

## CK-30 Shareholder loan not repaid within one year after year end
- Signal: balance sheet accounts named "Shareholder Loan", "Due from shareholder", "Related party"; a debit balance (owed to the corporation) at year end that grew without a matching salary or dividend. Also personal card charges coded here. https://www.connectcpa.ca/blog/shareholder-loans-the-one-year-rule-and-the-subsection-15-2-trap
- Prior return: last year's Schedule 100 debit balance and its date of origin; Schedule 9/T2 loan question.
- [fact] The test is one year after the end of the lender's tax year; repayments apply first to the oldest loan (FIFO) unless facts show otherwise (Folio paras 1.71, 1.81). https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/technical-information/income-tax/income-tax-folios-index/series-3-property-investments-savings-plans/folio-1-shares-shareholders-security-transactions/income-tax-folio-s3-f1-c1-shareholder-loans-debts.html
- False positives: a credit balance (company owes the owner) is not a loan to the owner; reimbursable expenses the owner paid personally net against the debit; loans to employees who are not shareholders and made because of employment; amounts cleared by a declared bonus or dividend (para 1.86).
- CRA adjusts: full principal into the shareholder's income in the year of the loan, plus s.80.4 interest benefit; later deduction under 20(1)(j) on repayment. [fact] connectcpa and mackisen pages.
- Example: Dec 31 year end. 15 Mar 2025 owner draws 30,000; 2 Sep 2025 draws 12,000. Year-end 2025 debit 42,000. Deadline for both draws is 31 Dec 2026. At 31 Dec 2026 the books show 42,000 still owing: flag; 42,000 is income for 2025. If 30,000 repaid by 20 Dec 2026, FIFO clears the March draw and 12,000 stays flagged.
- Noise: low to medium. Cut it by flagging only debit balances older than the lender year end plus 9 months, with a separate "deadline in under 90 days" soft notice, and by netting credits automatically.

## CK-31 Repayment followed by a new advance (series)
- [fact] CRA treats a repayment as part of a series where a loan is repaid before year end and re-borrowed (para 1.84), but not where repaid by applying dividends, salary or bonus (para 1.86). Same Folio page.
- Signal: repayment within about 30 days before or after a new draw; a repayment paid from a personal line of credit or a corporate-to-personal transfer then a withdrawal. Circular pattern "repay 30 Dec, reborrow 5 Jan". [fact] connectcpa.
- False positives: repayment by bonus or dividend offset, then an ordinary later draw; small frequent repayments of a running expense account.
- Example: 28 Dec 2025 repays 40,000 by bank transfer; 6 Jan 2026 draws 38,000. Flag the pair; the 40,000 repayment is not accepted for the one-year rule.
- Noise: medium. Cut: ignore repayments by journal against declared bonus or dividend; match on dates and amounts within 10 percent over a 60 day window; flag the pair, not each line.

## CK-32 No interest charged on shareholder loan
- [fact] Interest-free or low-interest loans can give a deemed benefit under s.80.4(2) (Folio para 1.20). Prescribed rate is set quarterly (3 percent was quoted by connectcpa; rate must come from a dated data table, not code).
- Signal: debit balance above a threshold with no interest income from the shareholder account.
- False positives: loan to a person who is an employee for a home purchase or vehicle (other rules, s.80.4(1)); balances that were under 1,000 all year; loan repaid within the year with immaterial benefit.
- Example: average debit 42,000 for 12 months at a prescribed 3 percent gives a benefit 1,260, in the owner's income, no corporate deduction.
- Noise: high if run on every debit balance. Cut: compute the benefit and flag only if over a firm amount (suggest 250); the same row can attach to CK-30 so staff clear them together.

## CK-33 Accrued bonus or salary unpaid 180 days after year end
- [fact] If still unpaid on the 180th day after year end the expense is deemed incurred in the year it is paid; pay by day 179. https://insightscpa.ca/bonus-accrual-179-day-rule-owner-manager-canada-2026/ (day count; note "Day one is the day after year end").
- Signal: accrued liability or "due to shareholder" growing near year end; director resolution; no bank payment. [fact] same page.
- False positives: credit to shareholder account counts as payment only if unconditional and drawable; vacation pay and SDA amounts are excluded. A T4 alone does not prove payment (same page).
- [guess, snippet only] CRA has an administrative position not to apply 78(4) where payroll is remitted, a T4 issued and the employee reports it. Conflicts in tone with the page above. Needs a primary source (IT-109R2 archived: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/it109r2/archived-unpaid-amounts.html , not opened).
- Example: bonus 60,000 accrued at 31 Dec 2025; paid 15 Jul 2026 (day 196). Deduction moves to 2026: 2025 taxable income up 60,000.
- Noise: low. Keep the day count in code with the pinned clock and show "day N of 180".

## CK-34 Specified investment business (five or fewer full-time employees)
- [fact] Principal purpose is income from property (interest, rent, dividends, royalties) unless more than five full-time employees throughout the year; consequence: no small business deduction. https://www.taxtips.ca/smallbusiness/specified-investment-business.htm
- Signal: rent, interest or royalty accounts; payroll count from T4 summary or QBO payroll.
- False positives: rent or interest from an associated corporation deducted against its active income is deemed active; rent that is incidental to the active business; a property manager's staff do not count unless associated-corporation services rule applies (snippet only, CTF page not opened).
- Example: holding company with rent 90,000, one part-time bookkeeper, SBD claimed on 90,000: flag; SBD denied, investment-rate tax applies.
- Noise: low to medium. Cut: test only where property income is over 10 percent of revenue, and mark "associated payer" from the related-party list.

## CK-35 Personal services business signs
- [fact] Four tests: owner holds 10 percent or more, would be an employee if unincorporated, not more than five full-time employees, payer not an associated corporation. Consequences: no SBD, restricted expenses, extra 5 percent tax. https://www.knowledgebureau.com/site/KBR/audit-potential-personal-services-businesses
- [fact] Same page: in a CRA pilot, 84 percent of studied PSBs wrongly claimed the SBD; they also missed the 5 percent tax and claimed barred expenses.
- Signal: one client above about 80 percent of revenue (rule of thumb [guess]), revenue code "consulting", no employees, no own tools, hourly billing, contract showing control.
- False positives: several clients; a real subcontractor with staff; associated-corporation payer.
- Example: IT contractor Ltd., revenue 180,000 all from one bank, hourly, bank laptop and badge, no staff. Flag; preparer records the control and tools facts and the CPA decides.
- Noise: medium. Cut: ask a fixed three-question note (client count, who controls, staff) and require it before clearing; never auto-decide.

## CK-36 Passive income grind
- [fact, weak: snippet only] Above 50,000 of adjusted aggregate investment income in the prior year the business limit is cut 5 dollars per 1 dollar; gone at 150,000. https://www.wolterskluwer.com/en-ca/expert-insights/ccpc-passive-investment-income-saga-comes-end (not opened, 403). Associated corporations are grouped (inference).
- Signal: interest, non-active rent, taxable capital gains, dividends from non-connected companies in the prior and associated returns.
- Example: prior-year AAII 70,000. Cut 20,000 x 5 = 100,000; limit 500,000 less 100,000 = 400,000. Plus check taxable capital cut, take the greater.
- False positives: income from a connected corporation; capital gains not yet realised; gains offset by losses.
- Noise: low. The main risk is a missing associated-corporation figure, not noise.

## CK-37 Foreign reporting
- [fact, snippet] T1135 when cost of specified foreign property exceeds 100,000 at any time; cost not market value; 100,000 to 250,000 uses the short form. BLG confirms the cost test and a stronger audit push. https://www.blg.com/en/insights/2026/01/foreign-assets-under-the-microscope-navigating-todays-t1135-audit-push
- Signal: a US-dollar bank or brokerage account, US-listed shares or ETFs, foreign rent, foreign customers' deposits, foreign withholding tax lines. T106 and T1134 thresholds: not found in sources opened.
- False positives: foreign property used in an active business; Canadian-listed ETFs holding foreign shares; foreign currency held in a Canadian account.
- Example: corporate brokerage holds US shares cost 130,000 CAD at year end; flag T1135 required, penalty 25 per day to 2,500 if late (snippet).
- Noise: low, but misses are expensive. Ask the question every year.

## CK-38 Implied slips (T5, T4)
- [fact] Dividends paid are on T5 (Schedule 3, line 710 schedule 55 setup per DT Max help; http://support.drtax.ca/dtmax/eng/kb/dtmax/keywords/rc/curr/t2/rc/t2f_710.htm). T4 follows the calendar year of payment, not the fiscal year of deduction (insightscpa bonus page).
- Signal: dividends declared in the retained earnings note, wages in the P and L, no slip counts in CRA data.
- False positive: bonus accrued in 2025 but paid in 2026 belongs on the 2026 T4, not 2025.
- Example: dividends paid 50,000 in 2025, no T5 on the CRA record by 28 Feb 2026: flag.
- Noise: low if the calendar-year rule is coded.

## CK-39 Line moved 25 percent or more with no note
- [fact] "A big drop in reported income or a sudden spike in expenses without explanation is a red flag" (huangcpas page). No Taxprep or CPA Ontario variance threshold page found; the 25 percent is the firm's own.
- Noise: the highest of all flags (every small line swings). Cut: require both 25 percent and a dollar floor (suggest 2,000 or 5 percent of revenue, whichever is more); roll related lines up (wages with CPP/EI); skip lines that are a flagged-elsewhere item; carry a preparer note forward when the same driver repeats.
- Example: advertising 3,000 to 9,500 (up 217 percent, 6,500 over the floor): flag; revenue lines 410,000 to 430,000 (5 percent): no flag.

## CK-41 Eligible dividends over GRIP; capital dividend with no election
- [fact] Excess designation: Part III.1 tax of 20 percent of the excess; alternative is the 185.1(2) election to treat the excess as an ordinary dividend with shareholder consent; GRIP is measured at year end; CDA excess 60 percent (s.184(2)); capital dividend needs the 83(2) election (T2054). https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/technical-information/income-tax/income-tax-folios-index/series-3-property-investments-savings-plans/series-3-property-investments-savings-plan-folio-2-dividends/income-tax-folio-s3-f2-c2-taxable-dividends-corporations-resident-canada.html
- Signal: Schedule 53 closing GRIP less eligible dividends paid in the year; minutes of dividends marked "eligible"; any capital dividend without a T2054 on file.
- False positives: opening GRIP is an estimate, not last NOA; GRIP is year-end so a mid-year over-designation can cure itself; wrongly flagging non-eligible dividends.
- Example: GRIP 18,000 at year end; eligible dividend paid 25,000. Excess 7,000; tax 1,400; or elect 185.1(2).
- Noise: low; flag only with a dollar excess. Use the CRA My Business Account GRIP figure as the check (DT Max help advises this).

## CK-42 Personal-looking expenses
- Signal: meals, travel, auto, home office, cell, club dues, clothing, groceries on the corporate card (mackisen, huangcpas). Vehicles at 100 percent business use with no log; meals deducted in full instead of 50 percent.
- False positives: grocery and retail charges that are staff meals or client gifts with a receipt; home office with a floor plan.
- What CRA adjusts most: [fact] these categories, per the pages above. Each becomes a shareholder benefit (15(1)) and lost deduction.
- Example: 9,200 in "meals" has 3 charges at supermarkets totalling 1,100 and two on Sundays: raise 1,100 as a likely benefit, ask for receipts.
- Noise: high. Cut: flag by merchant type and amount band, group by vendor, remember a cleared vendor for the year.

## Noise ranking and how to cut it
1. CK-39 and CK-42 are noisiest; use dollar floors, roll-ups, merchant memory. 2. CK-32 next; flag only above a computed benefit. 3. CK-31 and CK-35 medium. 4. CK-30, 33, 34, 36, 37, 38, 41 are low noise and high value; never suppress them, only add netting and day counts.
- Do not miss: CK-30 and CK-33 (dates); CK-35 (SBD denied); CK-37 (large penalties).
