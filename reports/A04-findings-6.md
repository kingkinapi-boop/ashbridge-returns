## Root cause (one): one deadline, two clocks
runner.ts:166 builds the deadline from `ctx.now` (the queue's Clock, read by jobs/runner.ts:29 after the claim) and engines.ts:214 compares it with the runner's own `now()`, which runner.ts:69 defaults to `new Date()`: the only product default to the system clock outside src/core/clock.ts, against testing.md ("the clock is the injectable one"). Which clock is the truth? The lease end is `lease_until` in the database (queue clock + lease); the handler cannot see it (HandlerContext has no lease), `ctx.now` is a snapshot that cannot tick, and the only clock that can end a poll loop is the runner's. So the deadline must be computed and compared on the runner's clock, and F10 must give the runner the same Clock as the queue so the lease and the wait share one frame (the 10-minute margin absorbs the milliseconds between claim and start). Anchoring on `ctx.now` buys nothing in one-clock wiring and breaks in any two-clock wiring.
Why the tests missed it: round 5 added a clock read to a path round 4's tests already covered. The round 5 spec pinned the runner clock in exchange.acceptance, exchange-safe-read and the db test (the db test even notes both must share), but left runner.acceptance's `runner()` helper (line 116) unpinned while `handlerMessage` (827-829) fixes ctx.now at 2026-10-02T12:00Z; fake timers there fake setTimeout only, Date stays real. Test 836 deliberately makes the two clocks differ and asserts the mix. The deadline was 3 Oct 11:50Z; the round 5b check's unit run finished before 11:35Z, the train ran at 12:21Z.
